# Design System: superfer

Character: a cyberpunk terminal that grew up. Dense, monospaced, dark. Opening it should say "everything is under control, here is what's next." Control comes from hierarchy and stillness, not from decoration. Neon is a signal, never a wallpaper.

## Color

Dark only, phosphor. Near-black with a cyan cast, darker than a monitor at rest, so everything lit reads as light on glass. Pure black is kept for the status line alone, the floor; the rest carries the tint so panel edges never vanish. Power comes from range, not from fill: the darks are deep, the lit things are bright and saturated, and mid-tones stay rare.

- `--bg` #030507: page background, the darkest layer
- `--surface` #0A0F12: panes, cards, list rows. One step up from bg so panes read as lit panels
- `--status` #000000: the status line only, the terminal's floor
- `--surface-raised` #11202A: hover row, popovers. Elevation is lightness, and here it leans cyan so raised things read as lit by the accent
- `--surface-top` #172A36: command palette and modals, the brightest surface
- `--header` #061319: the header bar, a deep cyan-black that frames the app in the accent's family without competing with it
- `--header-border` #123040: the header's bottom hairline
- `--border` #132631: hairlines between panes and rows. Low contrast, cyan-tinted, felt more than seen
- `--text` #E6FBFF: primary text, near-white with a cyan cast, the phosphor at full
- `--text-muted` #7FB2C2: metadata, timestamps, secondary lines. A cyan-gray, so even quiet text belongs to the screen rather than to fog
- `--text-dim` #3F6674: disabled, placeholder, seen machine subjects
- `--accent` #00E1FF: acid cyan, the signature. Only the focus edge, the primary action and the "next thing" cue. `--bg` text on an `--accent` fill is ~15:1
- `--accent-dim` #004A5A: accent at rest: bucket badge fill, selected-but-unfocused, hairlines tied to the accent. Text on it is `--text`
- `--signal` #FF2BD6: magenta, "this needs you": the unread dot, the list's `reply` badge (text on `--signal` at 15%), `needs reply` in the reading pane. Never focus, never a button, so the eye can tell "where I am" (cyan) from "what wants me" (magenta)
- `--info` #8C9EFF: periwinkle for AI suggestions and screener notes, and the far end of the radio and rail light. Off cyan so "the machine suggests" never reads as "you are here", and off lime so it never meets the personal account's green
- `--warning` #FFB000: amber for snooze and deadlines: `snoozed until …`, `overdue`, low-confidence classifications, shortcut conflicts
- `--danger` #FF4D4D: red for trash, spam, block sender. Semantic only, kept red so it never reads as `--signal`
- `--success` #00E1FF: same as accent. Sent, synced, done

Account identity uses hue, not a full palette: one hue per account, chosen at setup, shown as an 8px square (see Account square). Default set: `--account-personal` #39FF9E green, `--account-work1` #EDE95C yellow, `--account-work2` #C792EA violet. The set is picked so none reads as `--accent`, `--signal`, `--info`, `--warning` or `--danger` (closest pair: work1 to warning). The settings picker offers exactly these three. Account hues are never used for anything else, and never as text.

## Typography

- Family: `JetBrains Mono` for everything, UI and content chrome. One family is the TUI heritage, and monospace makes dense columns align without effort. Designed email bodies (own backgrounds, layout tables, responsive CSS) render in the sender's HTML inside the sandboxed iframe, framed by a hairline. Plain and lightly formatted ones (people writing to people) render as native text in the same iframe: no frame, transparent on the pane, `--text` at 13px and prose line height in this family, bold at 600, links `--text` underlined, quotes with a `--border` rule and `--text-muted` text. Only the part above the quoted history decides which. A designed email lays out at 600px at least (the width email is made for), then the frame scales it down to fit the pane, so its tables never squeeze; words break only when they overflow. Frames under 420px, the phone, keep the email's own responsive layout.
- Fallback: `ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace`
- Scale: 11 / 12 / 13 / 15 / 20. Body and list rows at 13, which is the densest size still comfortable for an hour of triage. 11 for timestamps and badges. 15 for the rail's labels, the app's map, read from across the screen. 20 only for the thread subject in the reading pane, the single place the eye should land. It holds at most two lines, the whole subject on hover; a leading `[owner/repo]` style tag moves out of it to a 12px `--text-muted` line above, since the list's group label already names it.
- Weights: 400 regular, 500 for unseen threads and active items, 600 for the reading pane subject. Bold at 700 is never used, it smears in monospace at small sizes.
- Line height: 1.45 for lists, 1.6 for prose.
- Tabular numbers everywhere so counts and times align in columns.
- Case: lowercase everywhere. Exceptions: the rail's items in sentence case (`Triage`, `Inbox`, `Work`, `Paper trail`, `Receipts`, `Settings`), because they are names of places, not commands; the radio's own labels, `Get in Flow` and `Flow Ongoing`, written as titles because they are a name, not a control label; and command palette section headers, 11px uppercase, because they are the only labels that sit between rows of lowercase results and must not read as a result.

## Spacing

- Base unit 4px; scale 4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32
- Rhythm: dense. Thread list rows are one line in fixed columns, `h-row` 32 on desktop and `h-touch` 44 on touch, every row the same height so the eye runs straight down the columns. Draft rows keep two lines at 56px minimum. Other control rows are 32px on desktop, 44px on touch. Rail rows are 40px, a place to aim at rather than a line to read. Tight within a group (4 or 8), one step wider between groups (16), pane padding 12. Vertical whitespace is spent only where the eye must reset: above the reading pane subject and around the command palette.

## Shape and elevation

- Radii: 2px on rows, badges, inputs. 4px on the command palette and popovers. Nothing larger, rounded corners soften and this interface is sharp.
- Separation language: hairline borders. No shadows. Elevation is a lighter surface plus a border. The one blur is the reading pane's action dock, frosting the email that scrolls under it. This is the terminal rule: every region is a box with an edge.
- Gradients are allowed as light, not as fill. Sanctioned uses, nothing else:
  - Pane depth: the reading pane background runs a barely visible vertical gradient from `--surface` at top to `--bg` at bottom, so long threads feel like they recede rather than sit on a flat sheet.
  - Status line: a 1px top border that fades from `--accent-dim` at left to transparent, tying the "next action" bar to the accent.
  - Radio live: the radio's label is filled with a linear gradient `--accent` to `--info` to `--accent` (clipped to the text). While playing, the gradient's angle turns one full rotation every 6s (`--duration-drift`), linear, infinite; paused or stopped it freezes at its current angle. With the logo cursor, one of the two looping animations in the app. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` it never turns.
  - Radio panel: its 1px frame uses the same blue/teal gradient; focused stations invert to a blue/teal fill with black text, and equalizer bars use the gradient vertically. These are confined to the radio.
  - Command palette: static radio-family lighting on the frame and search header only; the selected row is flat. No drift, pulse, equalizer or animated gradient. See Palette lighting tokens below.
  - Rail light: the rail is a lit panel in the radio family. Its fill runs from `--accent` 10% over `--surface` at the top to `--bg`, with an `--accent` 12% radial wash in the top-left corner and an `--info` 10% pool at the bottom-left. Its right edge is a 1px line from `--accent` through `--info` fading into `--border`, in place of the hairline. Rows span the rail's full width, no side margin, the label 12px in from the left edge. Rows are 40px with 15px labels in `--text` and 18px icons in `--text-muted`, counts 12px `--text-muted` (`--text` when they ask for you); the bottom group's labels are `--text-muted` with `--text-dim` icons. The open view's row is a solid `--accent-dim` block with a 2px `--accent` to `--info` bar on its left edge: no light behind the text, since a bright wash under near-white text costs legibility. Its icon is `--accent`, its label `--text` at 500, and its count `--text`. A row under the mouse gets `--surface-raised` and its icon turns `--info`. The act-on and later groups are split by a 1px rule from `--accent` fading out to the right, inset 12px. Static: no motion.
  - No gradients on other buttons, badges, or text.
- Contrast is the other half of the character: hard edges between dark and lit. Unseen rows sit visibly brighter than seen ones of their kind (a seen person outranks an unseen machine by height and weight, not brightness), the reading pane is clearly a different layer from the list, and the palette is the brightest surface in the app when open. Mid-tones are avoided, things are either lit or dark.
- Focus: the focused row has a 2px `--accent` bar on its left edge and a flat `--surface-raised` background. No light behind the text: a wash under near-white text costs legibility. No outline ring on rows, rings are for form inputs only. A mail row under the mouse (thread, group, draft and triage queue rows) gets `--surface-raised` at 50%, half the focused step and no bar, so hover never reads as the row you are on.
- Selection: shift and up or down picks a range of rows in the thread list, growing from the row it started on. Once anything is picked, every row shows a checkbox (see Checkbox) centred in its 24px left gutter, over the padding so content never shifts; clicking it picks or unpicks that row alone, so picks need not be contiguous. A plain move or a click on a row keeps the picks, and the next shift range adds to them. Picked rows get an `--accent-dim` fill at 40% over the row background, never the left edge. The focused row keeps the focus treatment, so you can see which end moves. The status line shows the count at left in `--text` (`3 selected`) and the keys that act on it at right. Keyboard actions (archive, move, snooze, work, delete, spam, unread) run on every picked row at once with one undo; screener, unsubscribe and deadline take one thread and warn instead. Esc, an action or a view switch drops the picks. The list keeps its scroll position while picking and stepping through threads.
- The left edge belongs only to focus. Nothing else draws on a row's left edge: no account bar, no bucket tint, no unseen marker. Rows reserve the 2px (transparent at rest) so focus never shifts content. Account identity moved to the square before the sender.
- Radii exception: timeline nodes and the unread dot are circles. They are points, not boxes.
- Pane focus (desktop): arrow keys act in one of three panes, rail, thread list, reading pane. The focused pane gets a 1px `--accent-dim` hairline along its top edge, drawn inset so nothing shifts. No ring. The rail's keyboard cursor looks like a focused row: 2px `--accent` bar, `--surface-raised`. In the reading pane, the cursor follows the thread timeline rules (see Components). Phone has no pane focus.

## Motion

- Durations: 80ms for hover and focus changes, 150ms for pane transitions and palette open. Nothing slower, the app should feel like it was already there. The one exception is the arrival afterglow (`--duration-afterglow`, 1200ms), a trace fading rather than a move.
- Focus glide: the focus mark in the thread list, and the open view's bar in the rail, is one 2px bar (`Glider`) that slides to its new row in 150ms on `ease-snap` instead of jumping. It remembers where it stood across navigation, so opening the next thread or switching views glides from the old row. The first placement in a session lands without moving.
- Leaving rows: a thread an action takes out of the view (archive, delete, spam, move, done, keep out) slides 24px right, fades and closes its height to zero in 150ms, starting as the action is sent. If the action fails or keeps the thread, the row comes back.
- Arrival: a thread the view has not shown before in this session (new mail from the minute refresh, not undone threads) glows `--signal` at 22% and decays to nothing over the afterglow; its unread dot, in Work, pops in (scale 0 to 1.6 to 1, 300ms). The first look at a view only takes note. The mail views refresh from the server every 60s while the tab is visible, and when it becomes visible again.
- Reader boot-in: opening a thread types its subject in, left to right in 24 steps over 200ms, while the body fades up 4px in 150ms.
- Easing: `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, fast start and clean stop.
- Toasts: a new toast rises from its own height into the front of the stack while fading in, 150ms. A dismissed one fades out over 150ms, drifting away from the stack. The deck expanding into a list on hover, and collapsing back, is a 150ms move of each card. A swipe-dismiss finishes in 150ms. All on `ease-snap`. Undo is `z` or `mod z`. A toast that can be undone stays 10s and counts down the seconds left at its right end, 11px `--text-muted` tabular (`9s`); every other toast stays 4s. Timers and the count hold while the stack is hovered or the tab is hidden. The count changes text once a second, it is not an animation.
- Reduced motion: honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, drop all transitions and animations to 0ms. Toasts then appear, stack, expand and leave in place with no movement; the focus bar jumps, rows vanish, the subject appears whole, and the logo cursor holds still.
- Two ambient loops: the radio's drifting border while it plays, 6s per turn, and the logo's `_` blinking like a waiting prompt (1.2s, stepped). Nothing else animates on its own.

## Layout

- Triage queue token: `--triage-queue-w` / `w-triage-queue` is 224px. It stays narrow at desktop widths so the judged message dominates; the other folders continue to use the stored `--list-w`.

- Desktop: a 44px header across the top, then three panes. Left rail 200px with buckets and counts. Thread list 360px. Reading pane takes the rest. Rail collapses to icons below 1100px. The status line runs across the bottom. Mail locks document scrolling; the list, rail, and reading pane scroll independently so the fixed radio stays aligned with the status line.
- Triage desktop: keep the rail, but use a fixed 224px queue strip instead of the resizable thread list. Its position (`3 of 12`) and adjacent sender/subject rows show progress; the reading pane gets the remaining width. A decision removes a resolved item and opens the next one at the same position, or the previous final item. Seen state never changes queue membership or the denominator. The queue respects account filters and grouped twins.
- The queue header keeps Compose as a secondary control next to its position indicator.
- Header: see Components. It spans the full window width above the panes, so the panes start 44px down.
- Rail groups: two groups separated by a 16px gap and the rail rule (see Rail light), no headings. Every row shows its 16px icon before the label. `act on`: triage, inbox, work, snoozed. `later`: news, paper trail, receipts. The group names are for this document, not the UI. Sent, drafts, trash and settings sit at the bottom of the rail, pushed down by a flexible gap, in `--text-muted` (`--text` on hover, `--text` with the focused-row treatment when the rail cursor is on them): sent with no count, drafts with its count, trash with no count, settings last. The rail no longer lists accounts, the header toggles replace that section. The collapsed icon rail keeps the same order and gap with icons only.
- Resizable panes: the rail and the thread list can be dragged wider or narrower on desktop. Rail 160 to 320, list 240 to 960, and the reading pane never drops below 360 (600 at `rail` width and up, room for a designed email at its own width), so the list gives way first when the window is narrow. Widths are remembered per browser, clamped to the valid range on restore; a temporarily narrow window can shrink the rendered list without replacing its saved width. The collapsed icon rail and the phone layout do not resize.
- Phone: single pane, list then thread, back via a sticky header. The app header is 44px on phone and shows on the list only (see Header). The header carries `back` at left and `delete` in `--danger` at right (the one delete that is red at rest, since touch has no hover) (`restore` in `--text-muted` for a trashed thread). Touch targets 44px. Keyboard hints are hidden, since there is no keyboard; search opens from a `search` button in the list header.
- Triage phone: the queue is the list view; opening an item gives the message the screen, with the existing docked decision bar and a back path to the queue. Decision buttons share the dock width and truncate when needed; the header above states the judged sender names and addresses in full.
- An empty Triage queue shows `0 to decide` and `no senders waiting`; the desktop reading pane says `triage clear` only when the queue is empty.
- Home-screen icon: a `>_` prompt in `--accent` on `--bg`, square, with the prompt inside the maskable safe zone.
- Command palette: centered, 640px max for empty input and preview; searching uses `max-w-palette-wide` (1280px) to give threads three quarters of the width, with at least 240px for secondary commands. Top-aligned at 15vh so results grow downward. At `md` (768px), the two search panes sit side by side; below it they stack, threads first. Each pane scrolls independently within the available height, while the input and footer stay visible. Phone: full width with 8px side gaps, top-aligned at 8px.
- Action bar: sits in a dock across the bottom of the reading pane on desktop: a full-width strip of `--surface-top` at 60% over a 12px backdrop blur with saturation at 150%, lighter than the pane so it reads as glass even over a dark email, with a top hairline in `--text-dim` at 60%, `py-2`, the bar centred in it. The email scrolls under the strip, softened, so the bar reads as its own layer instead of floating on text. The dock is sticky and in the flow after the last message, so scrolled to the end nothing sits behind it. The bar keeps 64px clear of the pane's right edge so it never meets the radio; when it does not fit, secondary actions move into `more` from right to left.
- Status line: a 32px bar at the bottom of the window, terminal-style, showing sync state, account, and the active keyboard hint. This is where "what to do next" lives.
- Compose: a panel docked bottom-right over the reading pane, 640px wide, at most 70vh tall, sitting just above the status line with a 16px right gap. No backdrop, so the thread stays readable above it. Phone: a full-screen sheet.
- Settings: a 44px tab bar across the top, `--surface` with a bottom hairline, `px-3`: at the left edge the path `>_ superfer / settings` (13px 500, `>_` in `--accent`, `superfer` in `--text-muted` and `--text` on hover, a `--text-dim` `/`, `settings` in `--text`) so the page says where you are; `>_ superfer` is a link back to the mail, same place esc goes, the tabs `accounts`, `rules`, `keyboard` centred in the window, and `back [esc]` floating at the right edge, out of the tabs' centring. Below it the section in a `max-w-palette` column with the mail pane padding, then the status line. Each tab has its own route and `/settings` opens accounts. Phone: the same bar with the tabs at the left and `back` at the right, the path hidden (the tabs say it), no separate list screen.
- Radio: sits in the status line, centred in the window, on every page (mail and settings), fixed so playback survives navigation. It is drawn over the bar, not part of its flow, so the bar's left and right content never shift. Station list opens upward from it. Phone: the same place, a 44px-wide target in the 32px bar.
- Toast stack: bottom-left, 16px from the left edge and 16px above the status line (48px up plus the safe area), growing upward. It sits over the bottom of the rail (trash, settings) while toasts show, and never meets the radio or the action bar. Over an open compose the stack draws on top. Phone: full width with 8px side gaps, 84px up plus the safe area, clear of the docked action bar.

## Components

- Button: a label segment and, when the action has a shortcut, a shortcut segment attached on its right. One box, one 2px radius on the outer corners, one hit area.
  - Label segment: `px-3`, 13px, height `h-row` (32) or `h-touch` (44) on phone.
  - Shortcut segment: full button height, `px-2`, at least `min-w-key` wide, the keys in 11px `--text-muted` centered, its own `--surface-raised` background, split from the label by a 1px `--border` hairline. Multi-key shortcuts sit in one segment: combos (`mod k`) 4px apart, sequences (`g i`) 8px apart. Wider than keycaps because the keys are bare text, with no box edges to tell them apart. The segment is part of the button: hovering or pressing either half affects both.
  - Secondary (default): transparent label, `--border` border, `--text`. Hover: label `--surface-raised`, segment `--surface-top`, segment text `--text`.
  - Ghost: no border at rest, `--text-muted` label, segment as above. Hover: label text `--text`, border `--border`. Toolbar and inline actions.
  - Primary: accent-filled for the one primary action per view. Label `--accent` fill, `--bg` text, 500. Segment `--accent-dim` fill, `--text` keys, hairline `--bg` at 40%. Hover: label brightens to `--accent` mixed 88% with `--text`.
  - Delete: neutral at rest, styled as secondary (or ghost where its neighbours are ghost). Hover and keyboard focus: label text, border and segment keys turn `--danger`. Never filled.
  - Focus-visible: border `--accent` around the whole box, segment included.
  - Disabled: label and keys `--text-dim`, no hover.
  - Phone: no shortcut segment (keyboard hints are hidden below `md`), the label keeps its full width.
  - Anything clickable that has a shortcut uses the attached segment. Non-clickable hints (palette rows, status line, key map, list header hints) keep standalone keycaps.
- Keycap: every shortcut shown anywhere is a keycap or a button's shortcut segment, never plain text. Standalone keycaps are for non-clickable hints. 11px, 2px radius, hairline `--border`, `--surface-raised` background, `--text-muted` text, at least 16px square (`h-key`, `min-w-key`) so single letters are square and words like `enter` grow sideways. Vertically centered with the label it sits next to. Multi-key shortcuts are adjacent keycaps: combos like `mod k` 2px apart, sequences like `g i` 4px apart. Key names are lowercase and short: `mod`, `enter`, `esc`. On the `--accent` fill: transparent background, `--bg` text, `--bg` border at 40%. Hidden below `md` like every keyboard hint.
- Action bar: replaces the inline thread toolbar. A strip centred in the reading pane's bottom dock (position in Layout), `--surface-top`, hairline border, 4px radius, 4px padding, buttons 4px apart.
  - Large actions: label 13px, height 32. The first is the view's primary (accent-filled), the others secondary.
  - A 1px `--border` vertical hairline, 16px tall, 4px margin each side, separates large from small.
  - Small actions: label 12px, height 24 (`h-6`), `px-2`, shortcut segment 11px, ghost. `delete [#]` uses the delete style and is always the last small action. A trashed thread shows `restore` in delete's place.
  - `more`: a ghost 24px button with a `…` label, always last. Holds every action of the view not shown on the bar.
  - Per view (large, then small, then `more`). Conditional actions appear only when they apply: `unsubscribe [u]` with a List-Unsubscribe header, `unsnooze` when snoozed, `done [w]` instead of `work [w]` on a thread in Work.
    - inbox: `reply [r]` (primary), `archive [e]`, `snooze [s]` / `work [w]`, `delete [#]` / `reply all [a]`, `forward [f]`, `unsubscribe [u]`, `mark unread [U]`, `treat as person` or `treat as automated`, `mark spam [!]`. Every view with `mark spam` but triage and trash carries the treat-as row before it, when the thread has an inbound sender.
    - triage: `let in [i]` (primary), `keep out [x]` / `archive [e]`, `unsubscribe [u]`, `delete [#]` / `snooze [s]`, `work [w]`, `reply [r]`, `reply all [a]`, `forward [f]`, `mark unread [U]`, `mark spam [!]`. Let in and keep out name who they decide on, the thread's undecided senders (see Screener in REQUIREMENTS): `let in Nora Quint [i]`, `keep out Nora Quint, Teo Marsh +1 [x]`. Up to two names, comma-separated, each truncated at `max-w-judged` with an ellipsis, then `+N` for the rest, never truncated. The name is a display name, falling back to the address; the button's tooltip holds every `name <address>`. The key map and settings keep the generic `let in` / `keep out`.
    - news, paper trail and receipts: `archive [e]` (primary) / `unsubscribe [u]`, `move to`, `delete [#]` / `reply [r]`, `reply all [a]`, `forward [f]`, `snooze [s]`, `mark unread [U]`, `mark spam [!]`. `move to` opens a menu (same styling as `more`) listing the other movable bucket views, inbox, news, paper trail, receipts, minus the current one, plus Work. `m` opens the bucket menu instead, under the bucket badge.
    - work: `done [w]` (primary), `reply [r]` / `snooze [s]`, `delete [#]` / `reply all [a]`, `forward [f]`, `needs reply` (`no reply needed` when set), `deadline`, `unsnooze`, `unsubscribe [u]`, `mark unread [U]`, `mark spam [!]`. Done archives, so there is no separate archive (`e` still works and also finishes). `deadline` opens the deadline picker.
    - snoozed: `reply [r]` (primary), `archive [e]`, `snooze [s]` (reschedule) / `unsnooze`, `delete [#]` / `reply all [a]`, `forward [f]`, `work [w]`, `unsubscribe [u]`, `mark unread [U]`, `mark spam [!]`.
    - trash: `restore` (primary) / nothing / `reply [r]`, `forward [f]`, `mark spam [!]`.
  - The `more` and `move to` menus open 8px above the bar, right-aligned to their button, styled like the station list (`--surface-top`, hairline, 4px radius, rows 32, focused-row treatment on the row under the mouse or arrow keys), keycaps standalone and right-aligned. Esc or an outside press closes them and returns focus to the button.
  - Every action runs at once with an undo toast, like archive. Toasts always use the toast stack (see Layout), never the bar. Screener toasts name the senders like the buttons do: `let in Nora Quint, 1 out of triage`, `kept out Nora Quint, Teo Marsh`.
  - Phone: the bar docks to the bottom of the thread view, full width, `--surface-top` with a top hairline, no radius, `pb-safe`. Buttons are 44px tall; the large actions keep their place and labels, everything else goes into `more`, which opens as a sheet of 44px rows from the bottom. Delete stays in the sticky header.
- Toast: one card per notice in the toast stack (position in Layout). Built on sonner, with our own card.
  - Card: `--surface-top`, hairline `--border`, 4px radius, 360px wide (`--toast-w`, full width less 8px gaps on phone), `px-3 py-2`, 8px between parts. Message 13px `--text`, one line, truncated. No shadow, no close button.
  - Types, told apart by a 12px icon before the message, never by fill: default (no icon), success (check in `--success`), error (cross in `--danger`), warning (triangle in `--warning`). Error: something failed, with what to do (`archive failed, retry`). Warning: nothing broke, but nothing happened either (`nothing to undo`, `no unsubscribe link`). Success: a sent message, a finished undo.
  - Undo toast: the action's result line, then a secondary small `undo` button (`h-6`) and the countdown. Only the newest undo toast, the one `z` undoes, carries the `z` shortcut segment; older ones show the bare button. Undoing (button or key) dismisses that toast at once.
  - Stack: newest in front. Collapsed it is a deck: up to 3 cards, each older one 8px further up and 5% smaller, its content hidden, so only the edges peek. Hovering the stack expands it into a list, cards 8px apart; leaving collapses it. Older than the third fade out of the deck.
  - Phone: full width at the bottom, swipe right or down to dismiss. No keyboard hints.
- Work: a view, the things that need real effort after Inbox zero. `work [w]` moves a thread there and out of Inbox (and every bucket view), `done [w]` on a thread in Work archives it. Archive and delete also take it out of Work.
  - Order: overdue deadlines first, then upcoming deadlines soonest first, then threads without a deadline in the order they entered Work, oldest first. No group labels.
  - Rows: the usual thread row. A row with an unread reply is at 500 like any unseen row, with the unread dot before the sender. `reply` badge when it needs a reply; the deadline as a badge, `due <date>` in `--text-muted`, `overdue` in `--warning` once passed.
  - Rail and phone tab strip: the count is the total number of Work threads in `--text`; when any has an unread reply, the unread dot sits 4px before the count.
  - A snoozed Work thread leaves Work (it shows in Snoozed) and comes back to Work, not Inbox, when the snooze ends.

## Voice

- Tone: terse operator. Labels are lowercase verbs: `archive`, `snooze`, `let in`, `keep out`. Counts precede nouns: `3 unseen`.
- No exclamation marks, no "Great!", no confirmations of success beyond the status line. Errors say what failed and what to do: `sync failed for work2, retry`.
- Empty states are one line: `inbox clear`. Nothing else.
